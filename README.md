# Immigration Case Management Software: Vendor Comparison

The biggest split in this market is between **immigration-native tools**, which are built around a forms engine and case types, and **general practice-management platforms** that bolt on immigration forms. Most firms end up choosing between a strong forms engine and strong firm operations, so decide early which one you can least afford to be weak.

**How to use:** Add one row per vendor in each table. Fill cells with a score (e.g., 1–5) and a short note.

## 1. Forms Engine (usually the deciding factor)

| ------------- | --------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------- |
| _Vendor name_ | —                     | —                    | —                         | —                             | —             |

- **Form library coverage:** USCIS, DOS (DS-160/260), DOL (LCA, PERM), and EOIR forms if you do removal defense. Confirm coverage for your actual case mix, not their marketing list.
- **Edition update speed:** USCIS rejects outdated form editions. Ask how fast new editions ship, how you're notified, and whether in-progress cases migrate cleanly without re-entry.
- **Single-profile data reuse:** Enter data once and have it populate every form in a bundle (e.g., I-130/I-485/I-765/I-131). It should also carry across related cases like derivatives, extensions, and amendments. This drives most of your paralegal time savings.
- **Fee and supplemental handling:** The tool should calculate current filing fees correctly and add G-28, G-1145, and I-907 automatically.
- **Filing output:** Check PDF fidelity (barcodes intact) and signature handling. Also check whether it supports USCIS online filing or only generates PDFs for mailing.

- **Case status sync:** Does it pull status automatically from USCIS by receipt number, or rely on scraping or manual checks?
- **Priority dates:** It should track priority dates against the monthly Visa Bulletin and alert you when a date becomes current.
- **Expiration tracking:** Look for I-94, EAD, visa stamps, passports, H-1B six-year max-out (with recapture), and PERM recruitment and prevailing-wage validity windows.
- **Response deadlines:** RFE/NOID deadlines and EOIR hearing dates, with calendar sync.
- **Seasonal volume:** Bulk workflows for H-1B cap season, if relevant.

- **Data sensitivity:** You'll hold A-numbers, SSNs, passports, and asylum material, which has heightened confidentiality under 8 CFR 208.6. Require SOC 2 Type II, encryption, MFA, role-based access, and audit logs.
- **Ethics fit:** Check the vendor against state bar cloud guidance and ABA Formal Opinion 512 on generative AI.
- **Data portability:** Can you export structured case data (not just PDFs) if you leave? Ask for a sample export before signing. This is the most commonly skipped check and the most painful one later.
