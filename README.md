# Immigration Case Management Software: Vendor Comparison

**How to use:** Add one row per vendor in each table. Fill cells with a score and a short note, and keep a source link plus the date you checked it.

If you cannot find relevant information for a given cell, use `NS`. **`NS` is a finding, not a blank.** For a category a vendor competes on, silence usually means the answer is weak.

**Scoring scale used below.** Every scored cell is `score — note`, with a footnote to the source it came from.

| Score | Meaning |
| ----- | ------- |
| **5** | Documented in public detail, specific and verifiable; meets the column's strongest bar. |
| **4** | Clearly documented and real, but partial or missing some sub-elements the column asks for. |
| **3** | Vendor-stated with some supporting detail (help article, screenshot, demo), but thin or unverified. |
| **2** | Marketing mention only with no detail, or a clearly limited implementation. |
| **1** | Documented as absent, explicitly unsupported, or a known reported weakness. |
| `NS` | Not stated. No public information found after searching the vendor's docs, help center, app-store, partner-directory and review-site footprint. |

Scores reflect **what a buyer can verify before a sales call**, which is what this table is for. A product can do something well and still score low here if the vendor does not document it — that gap is itself the thing being measured. Treat every score as a starting point for a demo question, not a verdict.

All research was conducted on **2026-09-17**. Every source is listed in [Sources](#sources) at the end.

### Method and its limits

Read this before trusting any individual cell.

Research was done by searching each vendor's public footprint — marketing site, help center, developer docs, changelog, pricing, privacy policy and terms, app-store listings, partner and marketplace directories, review sites, and funding and ownership records. Every URL cited in [Sources](#sources) is a real page returned by a search engine, and no citation was invented.

**One constraint materially shaped this edition.** The environment this research ran in blocked direct HTTPS retrieval of external sites at the network policy layer, for every host without exception. Evidence therefore comes from search-engine-extracted page content rather than from pages opened and read end to end. That is a real difference in evidentiary weight, and it cuts two ways:

- **Scores are deliberately conservative.** Capabilities that would likely have earned a 4 or 5 from a directly-read help article are generally capped at 3. A low score in this table means "not verifiable from here," which is not the same as "the product does not do this."
- **`NS` is weaker here than the scale above implies.** In this edition `NS` means *did not surface in search*, which is softer evidence than a confirmed absence. It remains a meaningful signal — a published DPA, subprocessor list, trust center, API reference, or changelog is normally indexed and findable, so silence across a whole category still tells you something, especially for a category the vendor competes on. But do not read a single `NS` as proof that an artifact does not exist.

The cells most affected, and most worth re-verifying before a purchase decision, are the ones that depend on reading a specific document: **AI governance (§10)**, **security posture (§15)**, **data governance (§16)**, **public API depth (§13)**, and **current pricing (§18)**. Form-library counts and app-store details (update recency, supported languages) are also under-verified.

Treat this table as a structured, sourced starting point that tells you where to look and what to ask — not as a substitute for a security questionnaire, a sample data export, or a demo driven from your own case mix.

- **Form library coverage:** USCIS, DOS (DS-160/260), DOL (ETA-9035 LCA, ETA-9089 PERM, ETA-9141 prevailing wage), and EOIR forms if you do removal defense. Most vendors publish a form list or a searchable library. Count the forms in your actual case mix rather than trusting the headline number.
- **Edition update cadence:** USCIS rejects outdated form editions. Public release notes are the evidence: look for dated entries naming specific form editions over the last year, and for a statement that in-progress cases migrate without re-entry. No public changelog is an `NS` here, and a meaningful one.
- **Single-profile data reuse:** Enter data once and have it populate every form in a bundle (I-130/I-485/I-765/I-131), and carry across derivatives, extensions, and amendments. Help-center articles about "profiles," "smart forms," or "data reuse" reveal whether data lives in a person record or is re-keyed per form. This drives most of your paralegal time savings.
- **Fee and supplemental handling:** Whether the tool calculates current filing fees and attaches G-28, G-1145, and I-907 automatically. Fee schedules change — substantially in 2024 — so look for a dated commitment to tracking the current schedule, not just a fee calculator screenshot.
- **Filing output:** PDF fidelity (barcodes intact) and signature handling. Check whether it supports USCIS online filing or only generates PDFs for mailing, and whether it works with USCIS organizational accounts for H-1B registration and shared online filing. Online filing coverage is form-specific and changes, so get the list.
- **Filed-version history:** Whether the docs describe a locked snapshot of what was actually filed, dated and separate from the current editable data. This is the line between a records system and a PDF generator.

- **Hosting and residency:** Cloud provider and region, and whether data can be pinned to a specific country if a client requires it.
- **Subprocessors:** A published, dated subprocessor list with a change-notification commitment. An unpublished list is `NS` and a negative — it's the only way to see who else touches your clients' documents.
- **Retention and deletion:** Retention periods, deletion on request, and what happens to backups after deletion. Also employee and subprocessor access policy.
- **Export and portability:** Whether you can export structured case data — not just PDFs — and whether the format is documented. Ask for a sample export before signing. This is the most commonly skipped check and the most painful one later.
